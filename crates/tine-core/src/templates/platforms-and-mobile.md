- # Platforms and mobile
  - "Does this work on my phone?" hides two different questions: what changes because a **window is narrow**, and what changes because Tine is **running as the Android app** instead of a desktop one. This page keeps them apart and maps what current releases ship on each side.
- ## Question 1 — is the window narrow?
  - Below a 640 px window width — on any device, including a desktop window you shrank yourself — the left and right sidebars stop squeezing the page and become **drawers over it**. Dismiss one by tapping the shaded outside edge, using its close control, pressing **Esc**, or pressing Android **Back**.
  - At 640 px and wider — including tablets — both sidebars are persistent panes that can stay open together and keep their resize controls.
  - The boundary is width, nothing else: it follows the window, not the device, so the same desktop shows both faces as you resize it.
- ## Question 2 — which app is running?
  - Some behavior keys off Tine detecting a **native mobile platform**, whatever the window width. On a phone-sized screen (shorter edge under 700 px) the Android app hides the tab strip and disables split panes, so one page keeps the phone screen. Tablets have room for two documents and keep split panes.
  - The desktop apps (Linux, macOS, Windows) keep the full window model — tabs, pinned tabs, split panes, saved workspaces, and multiple windows — toured in [[Workflows/Keep context visible]].
  - In both cases your graph is the same ordinary Markdown/Org files, so these are interface and surface differences, never different content formats.
- ## On Android
  - **Your graph is a real folder.** On first run you grant "All files access" and pick the graph folder (a Syncthing folder works), and Tine reads and writes that folder directly — coexisting with the Logseq mobile app on one graph. The file watcher polls there by default (Settings → **Files** → **Watch for external edits**), so external edits appear shortly after they land.
  - **An editing toolbar docks above the on-screen keyboard** with the actions a keyboard lacks: Outdent / Indent, Move block up / down, Soft newline, TODO, **Camera**, **Voice memo**, Undo / Redo, Date picker, `[[ ]]` and `(( ))`, the Slash menu, and a hide-keyboard button. Undo uses an arrow bending left; Redo uses an arrow bending right.
  - **Camera and Voice memo** capture a photo or an `.m4a` recording straight into the graph's `assets/` folder and insert the link at the caret — permission-prompted on first use. One that finishes after you switch graphs is saved into the graph it was started in and not inserted; Tine names the graph and the file.
  - **Share to Tine from any app.** Pick **Tine** in Android's share sheet to send text, a link, or one or more images. The item becomes a new block at the bottom of today's journal, the same place quick capture files to, and Tine shows **Captured to today's journal**. Shared images are copied into the graph's `assets/` folder. If Tine can't add the item yet, for example because no graph is open or saving fails, it keeps the item and tries again the next time you open or return to Tine. Retrying never adds the same item twice.
  - **Launcher shortcuts and a Quick Settings tile.** Long-press Tine's launcher icon for **Today**, **Quick capture** and **Search**. Add the **Quick capture** tile from the Quick Settings editor. **Quick capture** opens today's journal with an empty block at the bottom, ready for typing.
  - **Hardware Back navigates within Tine** — dismissing a dialog or drawer first and stepping back through page history, exiting to the system only at the root.
  - **PDFs use that same one-pane history.** Open a PDF link in place, press **Notes** to visit its highlights page, and use Hardware Back to return first to the PDF and then to the source page. Desktop-style PDF tabs and companion splits stay off on a phone.
  - **Plugins and themes follow the same lifecycle as desktop** — install (always disabled at first), review capabilities, enable, settings, disable, uninstall — and a package that did not declare Android shows **Unavailable** on it. The hands-on path is [[Workflows/Extend Tine]].
  - The whole interface scales through **Interface size** (saved per device) and the native status/navigation icons follow your selected theme.
- ## Touch gestures on a phone
  - **Swipe a block to restructure it.** Swipe a block **right** to indent it, **left a short way** to outdent it, and **left a longer way** to select it and open its action menu. A revealed arrow shows indent or outdent before you let go; it switches to a circled **more** icon when the swipe will open actions. An unavailable indent or outdent has no arrow. The left-swipe outdent band is 40–139 px; actions start at 140 px, giving you more room to outdent. Short or mostly-vertical movements are ordinary scrolling and do nothing. Swiping is off while you are typing in the block, inside tables, code blocks, queries, drawings and media, and with more than one finger, so it never fights text selection or panning.
  - **Swipe in from the left edge** to open the left drawer; a swipe that starts elsewhere, or that is mostly vertical, does not.
  - **Back is one ladder everywhere.** It dismisses an open dialog, menu or image first, then closes an open drawer, then steps back through page history. On Android the hardware or system Back gesture climbs that ladder and exits only at the root. Where Tine takes Back from the screen edge instead (iOS), the same left-edge swipe goes Back when there is something to go back to, following your finger and snapping back if you let go early; otherwise it opens the drawer.
  - **The image viewer takes touch.** Pinch to zoom, double-tap to zoom in and out, drag a zoomed image to pan it (keep dragging past its edge to reach the next image), swipe sideways to move between the page's images, and drag up or down to close. A single tap shows or hides the buttons instead of closing; use the **×** button, drag the image up or down, or press Back to close.
  - **Turn the block swipe off for chosen pages.** To stop swipes from acting inside blocks that reference particular pages (a kanban board, say), list their names in `config.edn`: `:mobile {:gestures/disabled-in-block-with-tags ["kanban" "board"]}`. A block that references a listed page, and everything nested under it, ignores the swipe. This is the same setting Logseq uses.
- ## What stays different on Android
  - **The always-on-top capture window is desktop-only.** The `tine --capture` desktop shortcut and its window over other apps have no phone counterpart. On a phone, capture with the share sheet, the **Quick capture** shortcut or tile, or iOS Shortcuts and Siri, described above and under iOS below. The desktop setup lives on [[Features/Quick capture]].
  - **One route at a time on a phone** — pages and PDF readers share one history, with no tab strip, split panes, or second windows; the desktop multi-window model does not exist here.
  - **Long-press a link** to get the same context menu a desktop right-click gives; a quick tap still follows the link, and text selection stays with the system.
  - **Export to PDF is desktop-only** — printing is unavailable inside a mobile WebView, so the page menu does not offer it here.
  - **Updates come through your install channel.** The in-app update checker and installer are off on mobile: a sideloaded release-signed APK is replaced by taking each new APK from the GitHub releases page, and an F-Droid install updates through F-Droid. The F-Droid build omits the network plugin/theme catalogue and keeps local sideloading.
- ## On desktop (Linux, macOS, Windows)
  - Everything the phone skips is here: tabs with pin, reorder, and reopen, split panes, saved workspaces, multiple windows, the modifier-click gestures, and global quick capture. Releases cover Linux and Windows on x64 and ARM64, and macOS as one universal app.
  - **In-app updates** run on Windows and Linux — the app checks once per launch and Settings → **About** → **Check for updates** downloads, installs, and relaunches in place. On macOS the same notice opens the releases page instead: the macOS build is signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens without a security warning, but in-place updating is not enabled there yet — download the new version and replace the app.
    - Automatic checks are ON by default. In Settings → **About**, turn off **Check for updates automatically** to stop startup checks and update notifications on this device. You can still use **Check for updates** manually; turning automatic checks off does not change how package-manager or manual-download installs receive updates.
  - Any folder on your disk can be a graph — choose it with the ordinary folder picker; there is no sandboxed document picker.
  - **On macOS, Tine draws its window with the system's web engine** — the same one Safari uses — and needs the engine of **Safari 15.4 or later**, which means **macOS 12.3 (Monterey) or later**. Apps get the engine that came with macOS: updating the Safari app on an older Mac leaves the one Tine uses unchanged, so the app bundle declares 12.3 as its minimum system version. If the web engine is too old, Tine explains this at startup instead of opening half-working. Opening PDFs needs Safari 17.4 or later; the rest of Tine does not.
- ## iOS today
  - A **public beta** is on [TestFlight](https://testflight.apple.com/join/rpGGpTVW): install Apple's TestFlight app, then open that link. There is no App Store listing yet.
  - **Graphs live in the app's own folders.** Open or create a graph inside **On My iPhone → TineOutline** or **iCloud Drive → TineOutline**; Tine refuses folders from other Files providers for now and says so. An iCloud graph is downloaded to the device before it opens, and a remembered graph reopens after an app update.
  - The iPhone and iPad app uses the same mobile interface as Android, so the notes above about one route at a time, bullet context menus and desktop-only features apply there too.
  - **Share to Tine.** Pick **Tine** in the share sheet to send text, a web page, a link, or up to five images. The sheet confirms **Saved to Tine**. The next time you open Tine, the item becomes a block at the bottom of today's journal, exactly as on Android.
  - **Spotlight finds your pages.** Pages of the graph open in Tine show up in iPhone search by title, with a short excerpt, and tapping one opens it in Tine. Hidden pages are never added. Only the current graph is indexed, and switching graphs replaces the index.
  - **Shortcuts, Siri and the Home Screen.** The Shortcuts app and Siri offer **Add to Tine journal** (it saves without opening Tine, and the block appears the next time you open it), **Search Tine** and **Open page**. Long-press the app icon for **Today's journal**, **Quick capture** and **Search**.
  - **Plugins do not run on iOS yet.** Themes work; downloadable WebAssembly plugins stay off until Tine has the extra review surface Apple requires for them.
- ## Shaping a shared item
  - Shared items follow Logseq's mobile rules. A link becomes a titled link, YouTube and similar video links become `{{video …}}`, and posts on X or Twitter become `{{twitter …}}`. Text highlighted in a browser goes before its link, and images follow as asset links, one per line.
  - The block uses Logseq's `:quick-capture-templates` from `config.edn`. The default text template is `**{time}** [[quick capture]]: {text} {url}`, and `:media` sets the template for a single shared image. `{time}` is when you shared the item, and `{date}` is today's journal date.
  - The same destinations open from `tine://today`, `tine://capture`, `tine://search?q=…` and `tine://page/<name>` (a page of the open graph). Each one only opens something in Tine and never writes to your graph.
