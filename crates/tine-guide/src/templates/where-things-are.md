- # Where things are
  - A short map of Tine's main places, so the recipes in this Guide make sense on first read: the page in the middle, the left sidebar, the tab strip, the right sidebar, and Settings and Help. Each place links to the workflow that teaches it — nothing here is a rulebook.
- ## The middle: one route per pane
  - Each pane shows the **page or PDF** its active tab points to. Tine opens on the **journal feed** — one continuous, editable list of days — and the **Journals** item in the left sidebar takes you back to it. With `:feature/enable-journals? false` in `logseq/config.edn`, the sidebar shows your configured home page instead of Journals.
  - Everything you write is a **block** (one bullet). Blocks nest under other blocks to form the outline, and any block can carry a task, a date, a link, or its own id for references from elsewhere.
  - Press **Ctrl+K** at any time to jump to a page by name or to text inside any block.
  - Mouse back and forward buttons navigate page history on desktop. On macOS, **Cmd+[** and **Cmd+]** also go back and forward; dragging with a side button does not navigate.
  - A page in a namespace has a **Hierarchy** section below its outline. Click its title, or focus it and press **Enter** or **Space**, to fold or expand the paths. It starts expanded and remembers your choice for that page until you switch graphs.
  - The reading column has a width cap. Press **t w** to toggle wide mode, which drops the cap; Settings → **Appearance** → **Advanced** holds **Standard page width** and **Wide page width** if you want your own numbers.
  - If one region of the screen — the page, the sidebar, a references section, or the conflict panel — fails to draw, that region shows a small card saying so, with a **Retry** button, instead of leaving the whole window blank. Your files on disk are not touched by it.
- ## The left sidebar: your graph's contents
  - With Tine's custom desktop window frame, drag the empty space beside the workspace switcher in the sidebar's top row to move the window. Double-click that space to maximize or restore, just as on the toolbar. The workspace switcher and its menu stay clickable. Mobile drawers and system title bars keep their usual behavior.
  - At the very top sits the workspace switcher — saved window layouts, covered in [[Workflows/Keep context visible]]. Just under it, the graph-name menu switches between the graphs Tine knows, with **Open graph…** and **New graph…** beside them — on desktop, Shift-click a graph row to open it in a second window instead of switching this one. Right-click a row for the same thing as a menu item — **Open in a new window** — along with **Open here**, **Show in folder**, **Copy path**, and **Remove from this list**. The row for the graph you already have open shows the two open actions greyed out, so the menu keeps its shape wherever you click.
  - Below the menu: **Journals**, then fold-up **Favorites** and **Recent** (each appears once it has something in it), **All pages**, and the **Namespaces** tree, where `Project/Roadmap` nests under `Project`. Namespaces appears only when the graph has namespaced page names. Favorites and Recent remember whether you left them open or folded.
  - The **All pages** entry opens the full **All pages** view in the main pane: every page in your graph, however many, as one scrolling table. That includes pages with no file of their own, which exist because a block links to them, tags them, uses their name as a property or names them by a task marker or priority, as in Logseq; Logseq's built-in pages such as TODO and the priorities A, B and C are left out, as in Logseq, even when they have a file (search and links still open such a file). The **Backlinks** column is the number of blocks the page's Linked References lists, and it updates once you pause typing. On a phone, choosing it closes the navigation drawer. It opens with the most recently changed pages first: the **Updated** column shows when each page's file last changed (a dash when Tine has no time for it). Click **Updated**, **Page** (name) or **Backlinks** to sort by it, and click again to reverse; type in the filter box to narrow by name, and tick **Show journals** to include the daily pages, which also adds the **Journal date** column (click it to sort by date). Rows open exactly like sidebar rows — click for this pane, Shift-click for the right sidebar, Ctrl/Cmd- or middle-click for a background tab, Alt-click for the other pane, right-click for the page menu.
  - The **Namespaces** section keeps its tree in the sidebar, and its first row, **Open the full view…**, opens the full **Namespaces** view in the main pane (on a phone, choosing it closes the navigation drawer). The view shows the whole tree with room for deep structures and long names, and only the rows on screen are drawn, so a graph with tens of thousands of namespaced pages stays quick. The top-level namespaces start open; click a triangle to open or fold one, or use **Expand all** and **Collapse all**. The **Pages** column is the size of each namespace: how many pages lie below it. Type in the filter box to search every full path: matches are listed flat, each with the path above its name, so three pages called `Notes` under different parents can be told apart (clear the box to return to the tree as you left it). Click a name to open that page, with the same Shift-, Ctrl/Cmd-, middle- and Alt-click and right-click gestures as the All pages rows.
  - Every row is a whole-row click target: clicking the blank space to the right of a short page name opens that page, just like clicking the name itself. Shift-click a row for the right sidebar, middle-click for a background tab, right-click for the page menu.
  - Favorites can be arranged without changing what Logseq sees: choose **+ New group**, rename the plain-text label, then drag favorites or groups left/right to lift or nest them to any depth. On a touch screen, hold a favorite still for a moment to open its menu (the one a right-click opens), or hold it and then drag to pick it up and move it; a finger that moves first scrolls the list, and a tap still opens the favorite. Deleting a group keeps everything it held one level up. Tine stores that richer arrangement on an ordinary editable page while keeping `config.edn :favorites` as Logseq's flat list; [[Workflows/Find and revisit]] walks the safe path.
  - The **+ New page** button at the bottom opens the same Ctrl+K box; a name that matches nothing becomes a page you can create. When Syncthing, Dropbox or a merge leaves pages that need a decision, an **N conflicts** badge appears beside it — see [[Reference/Files, external edits, and backups]]. Press **t l** to fold the whole sidebar away, and **t l** again to bring it back.
- ## The tab strip: what's open
  - Every open page or PDF keeps a tab along the top, browser-style: click to switch, **Ctrl+W** to close. Middle-click anything that opens a page — a link, a search result, a bullet's dot — to park it in a background tab without leaving this one. PDF links open in a companion pane by default, and their tabs can be dragged between panes like page tabs. Tabs come back after a restart.
  - The strip needs room: in a window narrower than 640 px (a phone held upright, a small desktop window) it gives way to the **Open tabs** list in the left sidebar, which shows your open tabs, switches to one with a tap, and closes one with its ✕; drag its rows to reorder them or nest one tab under another ([[Workflows/Keep context visible]]). It appears once a second tab is open, on every platform.
- ## The right sidebar: parked context
  - Anything you want **beside** the current page rather than instead of it — another page, or zoomed into one bullet — goes to the right sidebar: **Shift-click** a link or a bullet's dot. Items there are live editors, not previews. Press **t r** to show or hide the whole sidebar.
- ## Settings and Help
  - Settings search also finds **theme names and descriptions**, including installed packages, and **plugin settings** by their labels, descriptions or plugin name. Choosing a plugin setting opens that plugin's form. Search uses the selected interface language and also accepts English terms of two or more characters; theme and plugin authors' text stays as they wrote it.
  - The Settings button (on the toolbar by default) (or press **t s**, **Cmd+,** on macOS or **Ctrl+,** elsewhere) opens **Settings**: appearance (Light, System or Dark, interface size, wide mode), editor behavior, journals, files, backups & recovery, the graph, plugins, help & diagnostics, and the remappable **Keyboard shortcuts** list. The search box at the top shows only matching settings, with matching words highlighted. Choose a result to leave search and reveal its control, including Advanced settings; the setting is highlighted for about two seconds so it is easy to spot. Names that match come before descriptions that match, and a result found through text outside its visible name shows the matching description, keyword, alias or English name with the match highlighted. A single character matches only the text shown in your interface language, not the English names behind a translated interface. Clear the search to restore the normal tab. In **Keyboard shortcuts**, search filters commands and keys within that tab.
  - **Themes** is its own Settings category, with Style, Typography, Color scheme and theme packages. **Appearance** starts with Language, Toolbar and Mode; its **Advanced** section holds Fine-tuning, Custom CSS and content widths. Settings search reveals these controls too. Tine's menus, settings and messages can be shown in English or Simplified Chinese (简体中文); **System default** follows your system's language. The choice applies at once, in every open window, and is remembered on this device. Your notes, page names, properties and queries are never translated. Simplified Chinese covers the complete interface catalog, including settings, queries, context menus and recovery dialogs.
  - On a phone-sized touch screen, Settings fills the screen and opens a **category list**. Tap a category to open its settings; **Back** or the iOS edge swipe returns to the list, then dismisses Settings from the list. The close icon dismisses it from either level. Search results open the category and reveal the matching setting directly. Desktop and tablets keep the category navigation beside the settings pane; the button beside search enlarges the dialog, and Tine remembers that size on this device.
  - The **?** button at the bottom right (or press **Shift+?**) opens Help: this Guide, the shortcut reference, the features documentation, the bug-report and release-notes links, and the Welcome screen. If you would rather not see the button, turn off **Show the help button** in Settings → **Appearance** → **Advanced** (this device only). Help is still there: run **Toggle help** from the command palette, press **Shift+?**, or open the **Help** list in Settings → **Help & diagnostics**.
  - Choose and reorder buttons in **Settings → Appearance → Toolbar**, or open **Customize toolbar…** from the toolbar's **···** menu. Drag between **On the toolbar** and **In the … menu**, or use each row's up, down and move buttons. Calendar, Journals, Share, Theme, Right sidebar, Workspaces and Settings are optional; left sidebar, Search, Back and Forward stay fixed. When chosen buttons do not fit, the rightmost go into the menu first, without appearing in both places. Your choice stays on this device and survives a restart. **Reset toolbar** restores the default. Until you make a choice, the toolbar keeps its usual responsive layout.
- ## On a phone
  - The sidebars slide in and out from their edges, with a fading shade over the page. Your system's **Reduce Motion** setting makes both instant; choosing a page navigates immediately.
  - Below a 640 px window width, either sidebar opens as an overlay on top of the current route instead of shrinking it — tap the shaded outside edge, press Esc, or use Android Back to dismiss. The left drawer ends with a fixed **Settings** row, so Settings is two taps away whatever the toolbar shows. By default on a 390 px phone the toolbar has no workspace switcher, so calendar, journals, Share and the theme toggle fit beside Search and Back and Forward; the switcher is the first row of the left drawer. Toolbar customization lets you choose a different set. Split panes are off on a phone-sized touch screen; open tabs are listed under **Open tabs** in the left sidebar.
