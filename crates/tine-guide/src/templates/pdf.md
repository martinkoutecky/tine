- # PDF annotation
	- Open a PDF that lives in your graph's `assets/` (or drop one in). On desktop it opens as a tab in a companion pane, keeping your notes visible. On a phone it opens in the same one-pane history.
	- Opening and reading a PDF changes no graph files. Its highlight sidecar and notes page are created when you first highlight or annotate; existing highlights and notes stay available.
	- **Outside PDFs (desktop):** use `![label](file:///absolute/path/paper.pdf)` in Markdown or `[[file:///absolute/path/paper.pdf][label]]` in Org. Click the link to open the file in the same reader, without copying it into `assets/`. Windows drive links such as `file:///C:/Papers/paper.pdf` work too; encode spaces as `%20`.
		- Outside PDFs are **read-only**: find text, zoom, jump to pages, and select/copy text. Highlights, area capture and highlight notes are unavailable until outside files have a stable annotation identity. Opening, scrolling and zooming write nothing into the graph.
		- **Relative links** work the same way: `![label](../papers/paper.pdf)`, `![label](file:../papers/paper.pdf)` or `[[file:../papers/paper.pdf][label]]` name a path from your graph folder, and `..` steps up out of it. A plain `![label](assets/paper.pdf)` link still opens the annotated reader.
		- A plain Markdown `[label](file:///absolute/path/paper.pdf)` link opens in your system's default app. iOS and Android keep their existing outside-link behavior; they cannot access outside files in Tine's reader.
		- The file is read only after you click its link. After restarting Tine, open the original link again to authorize the read. A missing file, permission error, wrong file type or file above 256 MiB shows an error in the reader with its path.
	- **Select text** in the PDF to highlight it; click a highlight again to change its color.
	- Hold **Shift** while dragging a rectangle on Linux or Windows, or **Command** on macOS, then choose a color to clip a figure or equation as an image highlight. Dismissing the color chooser writes nothing.
	- Use the reader toolbar to choose a persistent **Light**, **Warm**, or **Dark** page theme, and open **Outline** to navigate a PDF's nested table of contents.
	- Zoom, find text, and jump to a page from the toolbar. **Ctrl/Cmd + wheel** or a touchpad pinch zooms around the pointer. On a touchscreen, pinch with two fingers to zoom around their midpoint; use one finger to scroll. Your page and zoom are restored when you reopen the PDF. At high zoom, only visible page regions are rendered sharply.
	- On desktop, choose **Open another view** in the reader's **⋯** menu to read two parts of the same PDF side by side. Each view keeps its own page and zoom; a highlight made, recolored or removed in one appears in the other at once.
	- On a phone, select text or long-press a highlight to open its annotation actions.
	- Every highlight becomes a **block** on a notes page named after the PDF, so you can write underneath it, link it, and reference it like any other bullet — and each note jumps straight back to its spot in the document.
	- An area highlight shows its clipped image on that notes page, in a `((block reference))`, and in an embed. Right-click the rectangle and choose **Copy ref**, then paste into another page to cite the image. Click the image to open the PDF at that highlight. A missing image stays as an asset placeholder until it arrives through sync.
	- If the highlight file changes outside Tine, choose **Keep mine** to merge your edits or **Use disk version** to discard them. If the disk highlight file cannot be read, **Discard my changes** asks for confirmation, clears only your local highlight edits, and leaves the disk file untouched; an unused local area image may remain.
	- If an area image cannot be cleaned up after a highlight saves, choose **Retry cleanup** in the PDF viewer. Closing or switching graphs waits until that cleanup succeeds.
	- It's how you turn a paper into linked, searchable notes without ever leaving Tine.
	- For the end-to-end path from dropping a PDF in to quoting its passages, see [[Workflows/Research a document]].
- ## Create one yourself
	- 1. Put a PDF in your graph's `assets/` folder, or drop it into a page.
	- 2. Open the PDF from its asset link. On desktop, its tab can be moved into another pane or split. On a phone, use **Back** to return to the page with the link.
	- 3. Select text to create a highlight, or hold **Shift** (Linux/Windows) or **Command** (macOS) and drag a rectangle for an area highlight, then choose its color.
	- 4. Write notes under the generated highlight blocks.
	- 5. What you should see: each highlight is a normal block that links back to the exact PDF location.
